The vision

Every community already knows what it needs. We are building the instrument that lets a government hear all of it at once — and prove where the money should go.

The problem

Public money is allocated on instinct — at scale, today.

A complaint reaches one clerk and one file. Nobody counts how many other people reported the same failure, so local budgets follow petitions, party channels and guesswork. Every misallocation is spent before anyone can measure that it was wrong — and each unanswered report teaches a citizen not to bother again. Local bodies across India commit development spending every year with no instrument that tells them what their own population actually asked for.

The concrete idea

A civic intelligence platform for local development planning.

A citizen speaks a problem or a suggestion into a phone, in their own language — no form, no literacy requirement, thirty seconds. Our AI transcribes and translates it, groups it with every other report describing the same issue in the same area, scores that group against who is affected, identifies the root cause, tags it to a specific UN Sustainable Development Goal target, and routes it to the department and officer accountable for the fix.

The official receives a ranked development agenda with evidence behind every line. The citizen receives a token number they can track. The same map is public — which is what converts a complaint box into an accountability system.

What has already been done

The platform is built and operational end to end — citizen app, AI pipeline, official workspace, public map and statewide boundary data. It has not yet been opened to citizens or government outside the founding team; this raise is to put it in the field.

Working today: voice/photo/text capture in 10 languages · four-agent AI analysis chain · automatic department routing · anonymised public dashboard · statewide interactive map · official briefing PDFs.

Why this is a sustainability innovation

Sustainable development fails locally not for lack of goals but for lack of ground truth. A district officer cannot act on a national target; they act on what they believe their area needs. Prajadhwani makes that belief measurable.

Every clustered issue is tagged to a specific SDG target — not merely a goal number — so a local body can report its development spending against the global framework as a by-product of doing its work, rather than as a separate reporting exercise.

Unique value proposition

The only platform that converts unstructured citizen speech into a ranked, SDG-tagged, department-routed development plan — without ever revealing who spoke.

Added value over the alternatives

  • A planning instrument, not a complaint pipe. Every existing system moves one complaint to one desk. We convert thousands of them into a prioritised agenda a budget can be written against.
  • Voice-first and vernacular. Written forms in English or formal Hindi silently exclude the people with the most to report. Speech in 10 languages removes that filter.
  • Privacy by construction. Officials see a token, a problem and an area — never a name, phone or address, because those fields are never written to the data an official can read. It is a property of the system, not a policy.
  • SDG reporting as a by-product. Sustainability accounting falls out of normal operation rather than requiring a separate audit.
  • Geography others must still build. Every district, taluk and ward boundary already digitised and matched to its representatives and departments.

Why now

Two conditions became true only recently. Vernacular speech AI reached the accuracy and the price point where a thirty-second Kannada voice note can be understood at scale. And India's digital public infrastructure normalised phone-based identity and location for hundreds of millions of people.

Neither was true five years ago. Both are now true everywhere in the developing world — which is why the same instrument, once proven in one Indian state, is not India-specific.

Barriers to imitation

The defensible asset is not the model — it is the boundary and accountability graph beneath it: who represents this polygon, which department owns this category here, and which officer answers for it. That is slow, unglamorous, jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction work, and it compounds with every area added.

Financial dynamics

  • Recurring by nature. An annual licence renewed by a body that has embedded the tool in its planning cycle; switching means losing its own history.
  • Target gross margin ≈ 85%. The only meaningful variable cost is cloud and AI inference, which falls per report as volume rises.
  • Land and expand. Entry at one taluk; expansion to the district and then the state is a pricing change, not a new build.
  • Break-even ≈ 400–450 paying bodies at the planned cost base — reachable within Karnataka alone, without a second state.

Principal risks and how they are managed

  • Government sales cycles are slow. Mitigated by pricing below tender thresholds and by entering at taluk level, where a single officer can decide.
  • Adoption depends on citizens speaking up. Field onboarding is funded as a first-class cost line, not assumed to happen by itself.
  • Data protection scrutiny. The anonymisation is architectural, and a security and DPDP compliance audit is funded in the first quarter.

Product roadmap — the civic intelligence layer

Listening  ·  built

What citizens are asking for, ranked by area, routed to the accountable department, tagged to an SDG target.

Predicting  ·  months 9–15

Forecast where water, sanitation and road failures will surface next, so budgets move before the breakdown rather than after it.

Allocating  ·  months 15–24

Match ward-level budgets to measured citizen demand and benchmark comparable areas against each other — the system of record for how a democracy spends locally.

The team

Two to three co-founders covering product engineering, AI, and government partnerships. The raise funds the first engineering and field-operations hires alongside the founding team.

Impact we will measure and publish

  • Reach. Reports filed per 1,000 residents in each covered area — the test of whether the voice barrier actually came down.
  • Responsiveness. Median days from report to department acknowledgement.
  • Resolution. Share of clustered issues closed within one budget cycle.
  • Equity. Share of reports coming from areas historically in the lowest quartile of civic spend — the measure that says whether this narrows a gap or widens it.
  • SDG accounting. Issues resolved per SDG target, published per local body.

Why an award accelerates this specifically

The binding constraint is not engineering — the platform is built. It is credibility with the first government customer, who is being asked to trust an unproven vendor with how their constituency is seen.

Independent recognition of the innovation shortens that conversation more than any feature would, and non-dilutive funding is best spent exactly where this plan is thinnest: the security audit and the field onboarding that turn a working product into a used one.
