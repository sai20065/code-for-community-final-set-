Prajadhwani.

Civic Intelligence · Bengaluru, India

1.4 billion people know exactly what their neighbourhood needs. No government on earth has a way to hear them at scale. We built it.

Prajadhwani turns what citizens say — by voice, in ten Indian languages — into ranked, routed work for the officials who control local budgets. India spends through 543 constituencies; almost none can prove what their people actually asked for.

The cost of the status quo

Public money is being allocated on instinct — today, at scale.

A complaint reaches one clerk and one file. Nobody counts how many others reported the same failure, so budgets follow petitions and guesswork. Every wasted allocation is spent before anyone can measure it was wrong — and each unanswered report teaches a citizen not to bother again.

What is already live

A working platform — not a concept, not a pilot deck.

Citizens report by voice, photo or text — no form, no literacy barrier. Our AI groups identical reports by area, ranks them against who is affected, and names the department and officer who own the fix. The same map is public — that is the accountability.

The loop, end to end

01 · SPEAK
Zero friction

A voice note in Kannada. A photo of a broken pipe. Thirty seconds, no training, no literacy requirement.

02 · GROUP
One voice becomes evidence

AI clusters everyone reporting the same thing in the same area and ranks it against who is affected.

03 · ROUTE
To whoever owns it

Four AI agents diagnose cause, draft the fix, name the accountable department and compile the briefing.

04 · PROVE
Visible to both sides

Citizens track a token number. Officials work a ranked queue. Neither can quietly ignore the other.

Why this compounds

  • Privacy is the product. Officials see a token, a problem and an area — never a name, phone or address. Enforced by data design, not policy. Citizens report honestly; no government can weaponise the list.
  • The map is the moat. Every district, taluk and ward boundary already digitised and matched to its representatives and departments. Slow, unglamorous, hard to shortcut.
  • Why now. Vernacular speech AI became accurate and cheap enough for this only in the last two years. India's digital public infrastructure did the rest.
